Why Self-Attention is Needed in Transformers

1. The Limitations of Recurrent Models (RNNs & LSTMs)

Traditional sequence models process text sequentially (word-by-word, from left to right), where each step depends directly on the hidden state of the previous step. This sequential nature introduces three major flaws:

 A. The Parallelization Bottleneck (Slow Training)

The Issue: Because step t requires the output of step t-1, the model cannot run computations in parallel.
Complexity: For a sequence of length n, the computational complexity is O(n) sequential steps. For example, you cannot calculate the embeddings for word 10 until word 9 is completely finished.
Hardware Inefficiency: Modern GPUs are designed for massive parallel matrix multiplications. Sequential processing leaves these powerful chips sitting idle, severely bottlenecking training and inference speeds.

 B. Vanishing Gradients (Forgetting Long-Range Relationships)

The Issue: As the distance between related words increases, the mathematical signal (gradient) used to train the model gets diluted.
The LSTM Limitation: Even though LSTMs utilize internal "gates" to better preserve long-term memory, they still suffer from the Vanishing Gradient Problem over very long sequences. The influence of early words inevitably fades as the sentence grows.

 C. The Information Bottleneck

The Issue: At each step, an RNN must compress all previous context into a single, fixed-length hidden state vector.
The Encoder-Decoder Flaw: In sequence-to-sequence tasks (like translation), the entire meaning of a 50-word sentence is squeezed into one final vector before being handed to the decoder.

> Analogy: It is like trying to summarize a complex novel in a single sentence—you are bound to lose critical details and nuances.


 2. How Transformers Solve These Problems

The Transformer architecture completely discards recurrence and replaces it entirely with a mechanism called Self-Attention.


[Old RNN Approach]       Word 1 ──> Word 2 ──> Word 3 ──> Word 4 (Sequential)
[Transformer Approach]   [Word 1, Word 2, Word 3, Word 4] Processed Simultaneously



 A. Massive Parallel Processing

The Solution:Transformers ingest the entire sequence of words simultaneously as a single matrix of data rather than step-by-step.
The Benefit: This allows the GPU to compute the relationships for every single word at the exact same time, reducing the sequential operation complexity from O(n) to O(1). This drastically accelerates training speeds.

 B. Direct Long-Range Connections via Self-Attention

The Solution: The Self-Attention mechanism allows every word to look at, score, and connect with every other word in the sentence directly, regardless of how far apart they are.
The Benefit: Because the path length between any two tokens is always exactly one operation, the model captures long-range dependencies naturally without any information decay or vanishing gradients.

 C. Positional Encoding (Replacing Recurrence)

The Solution: Since Transformers process all words simultaneously, they inherently lose the concept of word order (treating a sentence like a "bag of words"). To fix this, Transformers add a unique Positional Encoding vector to each word embedding.
The Benefit: This injects a distinct mathematical signature that tells the model exactly where each word sits in the sequence, preserving syntax and word order without needing sequential loops.


2. What Self-Attention Does

Self-Attention allows every word to look at all other words in the sentence and decide:
“Which words are most relevant for understanding my meaning?”
It lets the model pick out helpful context automatically.

Example:
Sentence: “Apple launched a new phone”

When processing the word “Apple”, the model must decide if it refers to a fruit or a company.
The word “phone” provides strong context, so “Apple” pays more attention to it.
This helps the model understand that “Apple” refers to the company, not the fruit.

Self-Attention basically allows words to use each other as clues, no matter how far apart they are.

3. Working of Self-Attention :

STATIC vs CONTEXTUAL EMBEDDINGS

What Are Static Embeddings?
Examples: Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText
Each word has one fixed vector.
“Bank” (financial) and “bank” (river bank) → same embedding.
“Apple” (fruit) and “Apple” (company) → same vector.
No sentence awareness.

money  → e_money
bank   → e_bank
grows  → e_grows
These embeddings never change regardless of the sentence.

Why Static Embeddings Are Not Enough

Word meanings depend on context:
“bank” in money bank grows
“bank” in river bank flows
Static embedding cannot tell the difference

Enter Self-Attention → Contextual Embeddings

Self-Attention transforms each embedding based on how strongly it relates to other words in the same sentence.
The update rule is:
                ei(new)​=j∑​wij​⋅Vj​

w_ij = attention weight from word i to word j
Vⱼ = Value vector of word j
(computed as 𝑉𝑗 =𝐸𝑗×𝑊v , not the static embedding)
This process turns fixed embeddings into context-aware embeddings, because each updated vector is a weighted mixture of the Value vectors of all other words in the sentence.


Sentence 1
money attends → 0.7 money + 0.2 bank + 0.1 grows
bank  attends → 0.25 money + 0.7 bank + 0.05 grows
grows attends → 0.1 money + 0.2 bank + 0.7 grows

Contextual Embeddings (Sentence 1)
y_money = 0.7*V_money + 0.2*V_bank + 0.1*V_grows
y_bank  = 0.25*V_money + 0.7*V_bank + 0.05*V_grows
y_grows = 0.1*V_money + 0.2*V_bank + 0.7*V_grows

Sentence 2 

river attends → 0.8 river + 0.15 bank + 0.05 flows
bank  attends → 0.2 river + 0.78 bank + 0.02 flows
flows attends → 0.4 river + 0.01 bank + 0.59 flows

Contextual Embeddings (Sentence 2)
y_river = 0.8*V_river + 0.15*V_bank + 0.05*V_flows
y_bank  = 0.2*V_river + 0.78*V_bank + 0.02*V_flows
y_flows = 0.4*V_river + 0.01*V_bank + 0.59*V_flows

Contextual embeddings solve the biggest linguistic problem:
One word = multiple meanings.
Transformers generate embeddings that depend on the entire sentence, not just the word itself.

Static embedding = dictionary meaning
Contextual embedding = meaning in the sentence (through Self-Attention)

Self-Attention answers one key question:
“When understanding this word, which other words should I pay attention to, and by how much?”

It solves:
✅ Long-range dependency
✅ Multiple meanings (polysemy)
✅ Parallel computation (no recurrence)
✅ Context-aware representations


4. Introducing Q, K, V (Query, Key, Value)

Until now, we saw what Self-Attention does:
It lets each word look at all other words and decide how much to use them to understand its meaning.

But how does a word decide which other words are useful?
To make this decision, Transformers give three different versions of each word:

a- Query (Q) — What the word is looking for

This is like the word asking a question:
“Who can help me understand myself?”
“What clues do I need?”

Example:
If the word is Apple, its Query represents:
“Help me decide if I'm a fruit or the company.”

b- Key (K) — What each word offers

Every word also carries a “tag” that represents what kind of information it contains.

Examples:
“phone” offers tech-related meaning
“tree” offers nature-related meaning
“money” offers financial meaning
“river” offers water/environment meaning
Keys tell other words:
“This is what I represent.”

c- Value (V) — The actual information each word provides

If a word is selected as important, its Value holds the information that gets added into the contextual meaning.
Think of Value as:
“If you choose me, here is the meaning I will contribute.”

Putting Q, K, V Together (A Simple Story)

Imagine we want to understand the meaning of the word bank.

Step 1 — “bank” creates a Query
Its Query asks:
“Am I a financial bank or a river bank? What clues do I need?”

Step 2 — All other words offer their Keys
“money” offers: “I'm related to finance.”
“river” offers: “I'm related to nature/water.”
“flows” offers: “I'm related to movement in water.”

Step 3 — The Query of “bank” matches each Key

Q(bank) matches strongly with K(money) → financial context
Q(bank) matches weakly with K(grows) → not helpful
Q(bank) matches strongly with K(river) → water context

Step 4 — Only the useful words contribute their Values

If bank attends more to “money”, its Value vector becomes financial
If it attends more to “river”, its Value vector becomes nature-related

This is how:

“bank” → finance meaning in one sentence
“bank” → river meaning in another sentence

Q, K, V allow Self-Attention to determine meaning based on context.

Why Q, K, V Are Needed

Before this step, every word had only one embedding vector.
But each word has three roles inside attention:

It must ask things → Query
It must represent itself → Key
It must provide meaning → Value

So the Transformer creates Q, K, V by applying three learnable transformations to the original word embedding.

This allows the model to learn:
What questions a word should ask (Q)
What information a word carries (K)
What meaning a word provides when selected (V)


5. How Attention Scores Are Calculated (Q, K → Weights → V)

Once every word has its Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V), Self-Attention must determine which words matter for understanding the meaning of the current word.
This happens in three clear steps.

Q = E × W_Q
K = E × W_K
V = E × W_V

These matrices are trained along with the whole Transformer.

5.1 Step 1 — Similarity (Q compared with K)

To understand a specific word (say “river”), the model compares:
Q_river → what “river” is looking for
with
K_river, K_bank, K_flows → what each word offers

This comparison produces raw similarity scores, which answer:
“How relevant is each word to ‘river’?”

sim(river, river)
sim(river, bank)
sim(river, flows)

5.2 Step 2 — Softmax (Scores → Attention Weights)

Raw scores can be:
negative
very large
unbounded

Softmax converts these into normalized weights:
positive values
sum to 1
represent “how much attention each word deserves”

w_river,river = 0.80
w_river,bank  = 0.15
w_river,flows = 0.05

These weights come directly from the Q–K similarity.

5.3 Step 3 — Weighted Sum (THIS IS WHERE V IS USED)

Once attention weights are known, the model gathers information from the other words.
This information is taken from each word’s Value vector (V).

Each Value vector is computed as:
V_word = E_word × W_V

y_river = 0.80 * V_river
        + 0.15 * V_bank
        + 0.05 * V_flows

Output (Y) = Softmax( (Q * K^T) / sqrt(d_k) ) * (E_static * W_V)

This is the moment where:
Q asked the question
K decided relevance
V provided the actual meaning

The final Self-Attention output is a linear combination of Value vectors because attention weights represent how much each word should contribute.

A weighted sum is:
the natural way to aggregate information,
smooth and differentiable (necessary for training),
stable,
and allows contextual blending of meanings.

Q identifies what information is needed,
K tells how relevant each word is,
V provides the actual meaning,
and the linear combination mixes these meanings proportionally.


6. Why We Divide by √dₖ (Scaled Dot-Product Attention)

After calculating similarity scores using Q · K, the Transformer applies Softmax to convert these scores into attention weights.
But before sending these scores to Softmax, we must scale them down.

Why?
Because without scaling, Self-Attention becomes unstable.

6.1 The Core Problem: Dot-Products Become Too Large

When Q and K are high-dimensional vectors (e.g., 64, 128, 512 dimensions), their dot-product naturally grows large.
Example:
If dₖ = 64, dot-products might be around ~8
If dₖ = 512, dot-products might explode to ~22 or more
Large dot-products cause issues:

A. Softmax Becomes Too Sharp
If the input numbers are large, Softmax produces outputs like:
[0.99999, 0.00001, 0.00000]
One word gets almost all the attention
Other words get almost zero
The model stops learning useful patterns
Gradients vanish (bad for training)

B. Attention Becomes Overconfident
The system picks one word as “the only important one”
Subtle relationships are lost
Meaning becomes brittle and unstable

6.2 The Solution: Scale the Dot-Product by √dₖ

To avoid exploding similarity scores, the Transformer divides by: √dₖ
This ensures the values passed into Softmax stay in a reasonable range, no matter how large dₖ is.
Example:

without scaling:   Q·K = 22  → Softmax = extremely sharp  
with scaling:      22 / √512 ≈ 0.97  → Softmax = normal, stable

6.3 Why Specifically √dₖ?

Because:
Dot-products of random vectors have variance proportional to dₖ
Dividing by √dₖ normalizes the variance to ~1

This keeps the distribution stable, and training becomes smooth and reliable.

6.4 Intuitive Explanation

Think of Q and K as huge vectors.
More dimensions → more random noise → larger dot-product
Softmax hates large, spiky numbers
Scaling by √dₖ “shrinks” the numbers to a manageable size
So the system keeps behaving properly regardless of embedding size.

6.5 Summary for Notes

Dot-products grow with vector dimension, which causes Softmax to produce overly sharp, unstable attention weights.
Dividing by √dₖ scales the values back to a healthy range, stabilizing learning and ensuring smooth gradients.
This simple trick is why Self-Attention works reliably even with very large vector sizes.

Why do we divide by √dₖ and not √d_q or √d_v?

Because the dot-product is computed between Q and K, and its variance grows with the size of K (which is the same as the size of Q).
Values (V) never enter the dot-product, so d_v does not matter.

The variance of this dot-product depends on how many terms are in the sum → that number is d_k
So the dot-product naturally grows in magnitude with dₖ.
That’s why we scale by √dₖ.

In practice: d_q = d_k always (by design)
In Transformers:
The Query and Key vectors must have the same dimension
(otherwise Q·K wouldn’t even be possible)

So d_q = d_k.


                    TIME COMPLEXITY OF SELF-ATTENTION
================================================================================

For a sequence of length n and hidden dimension d, the total time complexity 
of a self-attention layer is dominated by: O(n^2 * d)

RULE OF THUMB FOR MATRIX MATH:
Multiplying a matrix of size (A x B) by a matrix of size (B x C) takes 
roughly (A * B * C) total operations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE COMPUTATIONAL BREAKDOWN (STEP-BY-STEP STORY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ACT 1: THE TRANSFORMATION MOLDS(Linear Projections) --> O(n * d^2)
* What's happening: The raw words arrive. Every word must be prepared for three 
  different roles: a Query (what it wants), a Key (what it offers), and a Value 
  (its actual content).
* The Math: The input matrix X is (n x d). The three weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, 
  W_V) are each (d x d).
* Operations: n words * d traits * d mold dimensions = (n * d^2) per matrix. 
  For all three matrices, it is 3 * n * d^2. We drop the constant 3, 
  leaving O(n * d^2).

ACT 2: THE SPEED-DATING GRID(Attention Matrix) --> O(n^2 * d)
* What's happening: Every word needs to check its compatibility with every 
  other word to find the best context. The factory lays out a massive grid.
* The Math: You multiply the Query matrix Q (n x d) by the transposed Key 
  matrix K^T (d x n).
* Operations: n words * d traits * n other words = (n^2 * d). 
  This creates the final (n x n) attention scorecard grid. THIS IS THE BOTTLENECK.

ACT 3: GATHERING THE TREASURE(Applying Values) --> O(n^2 * d)
* What's happening: Each word looks at its row in the (n x n) scorecard grid 
  and collects its share of informational "treasure" from the Value matrix (V).
* The Math: You multiply the scorecard grid (n x n) by the Value matrix V (n x d).
* Operations: n grid rows * n grid columns * d value traits = (n^2 * d).

FINDING THE WINNER:
Total Work = (n * d^2) + (n^2 * d) + (n^2 * d)
In modern AI, the sequence length (n) is massive compared to the notebook 
size (d). The terms with n^2 grow the fastest, making the final complexity O(n^2 * d).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SELF-ATTENTION VS. RNN (THE CORE TRADE-OFF)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* THE RNN PROBLEM: 
  An RNN takes O(n * d^2) total math operations. While this looks cheaper for 
  short sentences, it requires O(n) sequential steps. You cannot process word 10 
  until word 9 is completely finished. This forces powerful GPUs to sit idle.

* THE SELF-ATTENTION SOLUTION: 
  Self-Attention requires more raw calculations overall, but it features O(1) 
  sequential steps. Because there is no step-by-step history dependency, the 
  GPU can compute the relationships for every single word at the exact same time.

SUMMARY: Self-Attention trades more total math (n^2) for flawless parallelism.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHY THIS MATTERS TODAY (THE d VS. n SHIFT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* THE PAST: When Transformers came out, sentences (n) were short (~512 tokens) 
  but hidden sizes (d) were large (~1024). Since d > n, the O(n * d^2) part 
  felt heavier.
  
* THE PRESENT: Today, we feed entire books or huge codebases into LLMs. The 
  sequence length (n) scales into hundreds of thousands or millions of tokens.

* THE QUADRATIC WALL: Because n is squared, doubling your document length 
  doesn't double your compute time—it QUADRUPLES it (2^2 = 4). 

This exact limitation is why the AI industry relies on hardware hacks like 
FlashAttention, or entirely new non-transformer architectures like Mamba 
(State Space Models) to get rid of the n^2 penalty completely.

Practice Questions: 

Q1: What are the exact dimensions of matrices Q, K, and V given a batch size 
    of 1, sequence length 'n', and embedding dimension 'd'? 
A:  * Q (Query):  (n x d)
    * K (Key):    (n x d)
    * V (Value):  (n x d)
    (Note: This assumes we are projecting into the same dimension d, meaning 
    d_q = d_k = d_v = d).

Q2: Why must the Query (Q) and Key (K) vectors have the exact same dimension, 
    while the Value (V) vector could theoretically have a different dimension?
A:  Because we calculate the similarity matrix by taking the dot product of Q 
    and K^T. For matrix multiplication (n x d_k) * (d_k x n) to work, their inner 
    dimensions must match. 
    The Value matrix (V) is applied later via a separate multiplication 
    (n x n) * (n x d_v). Since it only interacts with the scorecard grid's 
    sequence length dimension, its hidden dimension (d_v) could technically 
    be anything without breaking the math.

Q3: What happens to the gradients during backpropagation if you completely 
    remove the scaling factor (divide by sqrt(d_k)) in the attention formula?
A:  If d_k is large, the dot products of Q and K^T will explode to high magnitudes. 
    Passing large numbers into Softmax pushes the outputs to extreme values 
    (e.g., 1 for the highest score, 0 for all others). 
    The derivative (gradient) of Softmax near 0 or 1 is practically zero. This 
    causes the "Vanishing Gradient" problem, meaning the model stops learning 
    and the weights W_Q, W_K, and W_V will fail to update during training.


Q4: If you swap the positions of Word 2 and Word 5 in an input sentence, 
    how does a raw Self-Attention layer's output change?
A:  Raw Self-Attention is completely permutation-invariant (order-blind). 
    If you swap Word 2 and Word 5 in the input, their corresponding output 
    vectors will be calculated exactly the same way, just swapped in their 
    final positions. The layer treats the sentence as a bag of words unless 
    Positional Encodings are explicitly added to the static embeddings first.

Q5: Why do we use a weighted sum of Value vectors (V) for the final output 
    instead of just returning the raw attention scorecard weights?
A:  The attention weights are just a matrix of probabilities (scalars summing 
    to 1 per row) indicating *who to look at*. They contain no semantic text data 
    themselves. We must use these weights to scale the Value vectors (V) so 
    we can blend actual token features into a new, context-rich representation.

Q6: Why is Self-Attention considered O(1) sequential operations, while RNNs 
    are O(n)? Explain what this means for hardware.
A:  In an RNN, step 10 depends directly on the hidden state vector from step 9. 
    This forces sequential O(n) execution—meaning execution happens one word 
    at a time, lineally. 
    In Self-Attention, because the entire input sentence is packaged into a 
    single matrix X, the transformations and matrix products for all words 
    can be calculated in a single parallel step, O(1), utilizing thousands 
    of GPU cores simultaneously.

Q7: If Self-Attention scales at O(n^2 * d) time complexity, what is its space 
    (memory) complexity, and why is it a problem for long documents?
A:  The space complexity is O(n^2) because calculating Softmax requires 
    materializing and storing the intermediate (n x n) attention matrix in 
    GPU memory. If a sequence length (n) is 100,000 tokens, storing a single 
    float-precision 100,000 x 100,000 matrix requires roughly 40 Gigabytes of 
    VRAM just to hold a single layer's attention scorecard, causing quick 
    Out-Of-Memory (OOM) crashes.

"While quadratic time complexity makes processing long documents expensive and 
slow, quadratic space complexity is the true operational killer. Memory limits 
force an instant crash (OOM), which is why engineering breakthroughs like 
FlashAttention focus primarily on eliminating the need to store that (n x n) 
matrix in memory, converting a hard hardware constraint back into a manageable 
compute problem."

## 1. The Setup: Our Toy Example

Let's use a short 3-word sentence ($n = 3$):

> **"money bank grows"**

To keep the math clean, we'll set our embedding dimension to $d = 2$.

### Step 1: The Input Matrix ($X$)

First, we turn our words into static embedding vectors.

* **"money"** $\rightarrow [1, 0]$
* **"bank"** $\rightarrow [0, 1]$
* **"grows"** $\rightarrow [1, 1]$

Stacked together, our input matrix $X$ has a shape of $(3 \times 2)$ (Sequence Length $\times$ Embedding Dim):

$$X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{array}{l} \text{-- money} \\ \text{-- bank} \\ \text{-- grows} \end{array}$$

### Step 2: The Projection Matrices ($W_Q, W_K, W_V$)

The model learns three small matrices of weights to create our Queries, Keys, and Values. For this example, let's assume the trained weights are:

$$W_Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad W_K = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad W_V = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Matrix Calculations

### Act 1: Generating $Q$, $K$, and $V$ Matrices

We multiply our input matrix $X$ by each weight matrix.


$$\text{Formula: } Q = XW_Q, \quad K = XW_K, \quad V = XW_V$$

* **Queries ($Q$):**

$$Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$


* **Keys ($K$):**

$$K = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$


* **Values ($V$):**

$$V = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$



---

### Act 2: The Raw Scorecard Grid ($Q \times K^T$)

To find out how much every word matches with every other word, we multiply $Q$ by the transpose of $K$ ($K^T$). This yields our raw $(n \times n)$ compatibility matrix.

$$K^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\text{Raw Scores} = QK^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

> **How to read this matrix:**
> * Row 1 belongs to **"money"**. Its raw alignment scores are `0` with itself, `1` with "bank", and `1` with "grows".
> * Row 2 belongs to **"bank"**. It scores `1` with "money", `0` with itself, and `1` with "grows".
> 
> 

---

### Act 3: Scaling and Softmax

Our Key/Query dimension is $d_k = 2$, so our scaling factor is $\sqrt{d_k} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$.

To keep the math perfectly readable for your notes without decimals, let's look at **Row 2 ("bank")** to see how Softmax works.
The raw scores for "bank" are $[1, 0, 1]$. Let's skip the division trick for a brief second just to see the pure Softmax distribution mechanism:

$$\text{Softmax}(\text{Row 2}) = \left[ \frac{e^1}{e^1 + e^0 + e^1}, \frac{e^0}{e^1 + e^0 + e^1}, \frac{e^1}{e^1 + e^0 + e^1} \right]$$


Knowing $e^1 \approx 2.71$ and $e^0 = 1$:


$$\text{Softmax}(\text{Row 2}) \approx \left[ \frac{2.71}{6.42}, \frac{1}{6.42}, \frac{2.71}{6.42} \right] \approx \mathbf{[0.42, 0.16, 0.42]}$$

If we do this for all rows, we get our final, clean **Attention Weights Matrix ($A$)**:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.16 & 0.42 & 0.42 \\ 0.42 & 0.16 & 0.42 \\ 0.30 & 0.30 & 0.40 \end{pmatrix}$$

---

### Act 4: The Final Output ($A \times V$)

Now, we take our probability matrix $A$ $(3 \times 3)$ and multiply it by our actual semantic data, the Value matrix $V$ $(3 \times 2)$.

$$\text{Output } (Y) = A \times V = \begin{pmatrix} 0.16 & 0.42 & 0.42 \\ 0.42 & 0.16 & 0.42 \\ 0.30 & 0.30 & 0.40 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

Let’s calculate the new vector for **"bank" (Row 2)**:

* $\text{New Vector Component 1} = (0.42 \times 2) + (0.16 \times 0) + (0.42 \times 2) = 0.84 + 0 + 0.84 = \mathbf{1.68}$
* $\text{New Vector Component 2} = (0.42 \times 0) + (0.16 \times 2) + (0.42 \times 2) = 0 + 0.32 + 0.84 = \mathbf{1.16}$

$$Y = \begin{pmatrix} 1.16 & 1.68 \\ 1.68 & 1.16 \\ 1.40 & 1.40 \end{pmatrix} \begin{array}{l} \text{-- New "money" Vector} \\ \text{-- New "bank" Vector} \\ \text{-- New "grows" Vector} \end{array}$$

---

## 3. The Big Takeaway for Your Notes

Look at what just happened to the word **"bank"**:

* Its original static vector was $[0, 1]$.
* Its new context-aware vector is $[1.68, 1.16]$.

Because "bank" had high attention weights pointing toward "money" ($0.42$) and "grows" ($0.42$), its new representation absorbed $42\%$ of the information from the vector of "money" and $42\%$ from "grows".

This mathematical blend is exactly how the Transformer changes the literal vector representation of the word "bank" to mean a financial institution rather than the side of a river!
