MODERN POSITIONAL ENCODING
================================================================================

Read 03_positional_encoding.txt first. That note derives the sinusoid from
scratch and stops in 2017. This one picks up where it ends and follows what
happened next.

It is written as a story, because that is what it is. Nobody sat down and
designed RoPE. Each idea here exists because the previous one broke in a
specific, discoverable way, and someone went looking for why. Read in order, the
sequence is almost inevitable. Read as a list of methods, it is just vocabulary.

The cast, in order of appearance:

    2017  sinusoidal          the original, and the promise it did not keep
    2018  Shaw et al.         stop saying where, start saying how far apart
    2020  T5 bias             the same idea, made cheap
    2021  ALiBi               throw the vector away entirely
    2021  RoPE                ask the question backwards, get rotation
    2023  PI / NTK / YaRN     stretching RoPE past its training length
    2022  NoPE                the reversal: maybe none of this was needed
    2024  2D and M-RoPE       position was never one-dimensional

================================================================================

1. The Promise That Was Not Kept
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Note 03 section 7 recorded a decision. The 2017 authors tested learned positional
embeddings against sinusoids, found no quality difference, and picked the
sinusoids anyway. Their stated reason:

    "it may allow the model to extrapolate to sequence lengths longer than the
     ones encountered during training"

Read that "may". It was a hypothesis, not a result. The formula is defined for
every position, so a model trained on 512 tokens can be handed position 5,000 and
something will come out. The hope was that the something would be sensible.

It is not. This is the most important thing to know about sinusoidal positional
encoding, and it took several years to become common knowledge.

1.1 The Test That Fails
-----------------------

Here is the situation that will run through this whole note. Keep it in mind; it
is the reason every method below exists.

    A model is trained on documents up to 2,048 tokens.
    Someone pastes in a 30,000-token contract and asks what clause 14 says.

Everything about the model is fine. The vocabulary is fine, the attention is
fine, the weights that learned English on short documents still know English. The
only thing that has changed is that tokens now carry position numbers the model
has never seen.

What comes out is not slightly worse. It is often gibberish - repeated phrases,
lost tracking of who is speaking, perplexity climbing by orders of magnitude
rather than percent. The model does not degrade gracefully past its training
length. It falls off a cliff.

1.2 Why the Cliff Is There
--------------------------

Note 03 section 5.2 proved something reassuring: PE(pos) . PE(pos+k) depends only
on the gap k. Positional similarity is translation-invariant. So why does moving
to larger positions break anything at all?

Because that clean property describes PE alone, and the model never sees PE
alone. It sees E + PE, projected through W_Q and W_K, summed with three other
interaction terms (note 03 section 6.1), then passed through a softmax whose
output is a competition among all n positions. None of that is
translation-invariant. The four-term expansion holds at any position; what those
terms have learned to *do* was fitted on the positions that appeared in training.

Two concrete things go wrong.

The slow dimensions enter unseen territory. Note 03 section 3.5 built a
geometric ladder of wavelengths, from about 6 positions up to about 62,800. Train
on 2,048 positions and ask what each dimension has actually experienced:

    wavelength 6         wrapped 341 times     saw every angle, many times
    wavelength 600       wrapped 3.4 times     saw every angle
    wavelength 6,000     covered 1/3 of a turn seen only a slice
    wavelength 62,800    covered 3% of a turn  seen a tiny arc

The fast dimensions are safe: they completed their cycle so many times during
training that every possible value showed up. The slow dimensions are not. For
them, "position 2,048" is not the end of a familiar range - it is the furthest
edge of a small arc, and position 30,000 is somewhere on the circle the model has
never visited. Whatever those dimensions mean to the network, they were only ever
calibrated on a fragment of their own domain.

Remember this asymmetry. Fast dimensions safe, slow dimensions fragile. Three
sections from now it becomes the entire basis for how long-context models are
built.

The softmax has never had to divide attention this many ways. Trained on 2,048
tokens, every row of the attention matrix distributed a total weight of 1 across
at most 2,048 competitors. At 30,000 tokens it must spread the same total across
fifteen times as many. Even if every score were perfect, the distribution flattens
and the entropy of each row rises well past anything seen in training. Position is
not the only thing that goes out of distribution when a sequence gets longer.

1.3 The Diagnosis
-----------------

Strip away the details and the fault is structural, and it was visible in note 03
all along.

Sinusoidal PE is ABSOLUTE. It answers "which slot is this token in?" But nothing
the model needs to know is phrased that way. Grammar cares that the adjective is
one before the noun. Coreference cares that the pronoun is forty tokens after its
antecedent. Every rule the model wants is about GAPS.

Note 03 section 5 showed the gap information is in there - as a rotation M_k, and
as a dot product depending only on k. But "in there" means the model has to
extract it, through learned projections, and it can only learn that extraction
from the (position, gap) combinations training happened to contain. It learned
gap-of-3 at positions 0 through 2,048. Gap-of-3 at position 30,000 is, to the
weights that do the extracting, a new input.

So the fix suggests itself, and it is the thread the next three sections follow:

    Stop encoding where a token is and hope the gap survives.
    Encode the gap directly.

If the model is handed "these tokens are 3 apart" instead of "one is at 2,048 and
the other at 2,051", then position 30,000 stops being new information. A gap of 3
is a gap of 3 anywhere in the document. Length generalization stops being
something to hope for and becomes something the representation guarantees.

That is the whole plot. Everything from here is people finding cheaper and
cleaner ways to say "3 apart".

================================================================================

2. First Attempt: Put the Gap Inside Attention (Shaw, 2018)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first serious relative scheme came a year after the original paper, from Shaw
and colleagues, and it took the direct route.

If what matters is the gap, then position should not live in the input at all.
It should live where pairs of tokens actually meet - inside the attention score.

2.1 The Idea
------------

Recall the score from 02 section 4.1: `s_ij = q_i . k_j`. Shaw's change is to give
every pair its own learned vector describing their offset, and fold it in:

    s_ij = q_i . ( k_j + a_(j-i) )

`a_(j-i)` is a learned vector, looked up from a small table indexed by the offset
`j - i`. Not by i, not by j. Only by the gap.

The input embeddings go back to carrying pure meaning. No PE is added at the
bottom at all. Position enters only at the moment two tokens are compared, and it
enters as the answer to exactly the question the model was asking: how far apart
are these two, and in which direction?

The table is clipped. Offsets beyond some distance, say 128, all share one
entry. The authors' reasoning was that precise distance stops mattering once
tokens are far apart - "somewhere off to the left" is enough. Clipping also
bounds the table size and means any offset, however large, has a valid lookup.
Length generalization comes free, in the crudest possible way: everything distant
is treated identically.

2.2 What It Cost
----------------

It worked. It improved translation quality. And almost nobody used it, because of
what it does to the arithmetic.

In standard attention, `Q K^T` is one matrix multiplication: (n x d_k) by
(d_k x n). Every pair is handled by the same well-optimized operation, and the
Value blend is the same again. That single fact is why attention runs fast on a
GPU (02 section 8).

Shaw's version breaks it. Each pair (i, j) now needs its own vector `a_(j-i)`
added to `k_j` before the dot product - a different addend for every pair. That
is no longer one matrix multiply. Implemented naively it materializes a tensor of
shape (n, n, d_k), which for n = 1,024 and d_k = 64 is 67 million floats per head
per layer against the 1 million of the score matrix itself - a factor of d_k,
64x, on the largest tensor attention already has. Clever reshaping
recovers some of it, but the operation never becomes a plain matmul again.

So the lesson from attempt one is not "relative position is wrong". It is:

    Relative position is right. Paying for it per pair, per dimension,
    is too expensive.

Someone needed to find a cheaper place to put it.

================================================================================

3. Second Attempt: Make It a Scalar (T5, 2020)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

T5's answer is almost embarrassingly simple, and it is the moment relative
position became practical.

Shaw's scheme adds a whole `d_k`-dimensional vector per pair. But look at what it
is ultimately for: nudging one number, the score `s_ij`. So skip the vector.
Add the number.

    s_ij = q_i . k_j  +  b(j - i)

`b` is a learned SCALAR per offset, per head. Nothing else changes. `Q K^T` stays
one matrix multiplication; the bias is a small matrix added to the scores
afterwards, exactly like the mask of 02 section 7.2. The cost is a rounding
error.

3.1 Buckets
-----------

One learned scalar per distinct offset would still mean thousands of parameters
per head, most of them for offsets seen a handful of times. T5 groups offsets
into 32 BUCKETS, and the grouping is the interesting part:

    small offsets     one bucket each        1, 2, 3, 4, ... exactly
    larger offsets    logarithmically wide   ... 16-22, 23-31, 32-45 ...
    beyond the last   all share one bucket

Fine resolution near zero, coarse resolution far away. This is the same instinct
that made note 03's wavelengths geometric rather than linear: the difference
between gap 1 and gap 2 is grammatically enormous, and the difference between gap
600 and gap 601 is nothing. Resolution should be spent where it changes meaning.

The bias is computed once and shared by every layer, so the whole mechanism costs
32 numbers per head for the entire model.

3.2 What It Gained and What It Gave Up
--------------------------------------

Gained: relative position, essentially free, compatible with fast attention
kernels, and each head learns its own distance preference - one head can prefer
adjacent tokens while another ignores distance entirely.

Gave up: expressiveness. A scalar per bucket can say "pay more attention to
things about 20 tokens back". It cannot say anything that depends on both the gap
AND the content, because the bias is added regardless of what the two tokens are.
Shaw's vector, sitting inside the dot product with `q_i`, could modulate by
content. T5's scalar cannot.

And the extrapolation is still crude. Beyond the last bucket every distance is
identical, so a 30,000-token contract is, positionally, a 2,048-token document
with a large undifferentiated fog attached.

The score is now the right place. The representation is still not right.

================================================================================

4. Third Attempt: Throw the Vector Away (ALiBi, 2021)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper is titled "Train Short, Test Long", which tells you it went straight
at the problem section 1 posed rather than at translation quality.

ALiBi asks: if the bias is just a scalar function of distance, does it need to be
LEARNED at all? What if we simply write it down?

    s_ij = q_i . k_j  -  m * (i - j)

For a causal model j is never greater than i, so `(i - j)` is the number of
tokens back, and the bias is a straight-line penalty: the further back, the more
subtracted. Nothing is looked up. There is no table, no positional vector, no
embedding addition. Position appears nowhere in the model except as a linear
ramp on the attention scores.

`m` is a fixed per-head slope. For h heads the slopes form a geometric sequence
ending at 2^-8:

    8 heads:   1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/64, 1/128, 1/256

A head with slope 1/2 is punished hard for looking far: 20 tokens back costs it
10 units of score, which after softmax is annihilation. It becomes a local head,
reading essentially the last few words. A head with slope 1/256 pays 0.08 for the
same distance - almost nothing - and stays free to attend across the whole
document.

That geometric spread is the design. The heads are not given a distance
preference to learn; they are ASSIGNED one, spanning local to global, and the
model routes each kind of work to whichever head has the right reach. It is the
same "cover every scale multiplicatively" reasoning as note 03's frequency
ladder, applied to heads instead of dimensions.

4.1 Why It Extrapolates
-----------------------

Now the payoff. Take the 30,000-token contract.

Nothing in ALiBi has a domain. `m * (i - j)` is defined for any distance and
requires no table lookup, no unseen angle, no dimension that has only experienced
3% of its cycle. Feed it distance 25,000 and it computes a large penalty, which
is a sensible thing to compute.

Models trained at 1,024 tokens ran at 2,048 and beyond with perplexity that kept
improving rather than exploding. After four years of assuming extrapolation was
just hard, a linear penalty did it.

4.2 The Catch
-------------

Look closely at what extrapolation means here.

The far-away tokens are not being understood at long range. They are being
suppressed. Every head has a slope, every slope eventually crushes distant scores
toward negative infinity, so a long input is handled by each head reading its own
sliding window and ignoring the rest.

That is a RECENCY PRIOR, hard-coded and unlearnable. For language modelling it is
an excellent prior - the next word usually depends most on the last few. For the
contract question it is exactly wrong. "What does clause 14 say" needs a token at
position 200 to attend precisely to a token at position 24,000, and the head with
the gentlest slope still pays a penalty that grows without bound.

So ALiBi does not really solve long context. It survives long inputs
gracefully. The distinction matters enormously, and it is why ALiBi appears in
some strong models and yet is not what the long-context race settled on.

The scoreboard after three attempts:

    Shaw    relative, expressive, content-aware      too expensive
    T5      relative, cheap                          not expressive, fog at range
    ALiBi   relative, free, extrapolates             cannot actually use range

Each one is relative. Each one bolts the gap on as a correction to a score. None
of them makes relative position a property of the representation itself.

================================================================================

5. The Synthesis: RoPE (2021)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RoPE - Rotary Position Embedding - is the one that won. Nearly every open model
you can name uses it: Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma, DeepSeek, Phi.

What makes it worth studying is not the result. It is the method. Instead of
proposing a mechanism and checking whether the gap survives, RoPE writes down the
property it wants and solves for the mechanism.

5.1 Asking Backwards
--------------------

Every scheme so far has the shape "compute the score the usual way, then correct
it for distance". Turn that around.

We want the score itself - not a correction to it - to depend only on the gap. So
state the requirement as an equation. Find some function f that stamps position
into a vector, such that:

    < f(q, m) , f(k, n) >   depends on q, k, and (m - n)
                            and on nothing else about m and n

If such an f exists, relative position is not extracted, learned, corrected for,
or hoped for. It is what the dot product computes.

Now, what operation can you apply to a vector, parameterized by a position, that
changes the vector but leaves its interaction with another such vector depending
only on the difference of the parameters?

Note 03 already answered this, in section 5.1, without noticing it was an answer.

A ROTATION. Rotate one vector by angle `m*w` and another by `n*w`. The angle
BETWEEN them was some angle θ; now it is θ + (m - n)*w. The relative angle shifted
by exactly the difference of positions and the absolute positions cancelled. And
a dot product depends only on lengths and the angle between - so the dot product
of two rotated vectors depends only on the gap.

Not approximately. Not extractable-in-principle. Identically, as arithmetic.

5.2 The Mechanism
-----------------

Take the query and key vectors and cut them into 2D pairs, the same pairing note
03 used. For pair i, rotate it by angle `pos * w_i`:

    [ q'_(2i)   ]   [ cos(pos*w_i)   -sin(pos*w_i) ] [ q_(2i)   ]
    [           ] = [                              ] [          ]
    [ q'_(2i+1) ]   [ sin(pos*w_i)    cos(pos*w_i) ] [ q_(2i+1) ]

    w_i = 1 / 10000^(2i / d_model)

That frequency is note 03's, unchanged. The same geometric ladder, the same base
constant, the same reasoning about resolution at every scale. RoPE did not
redesign the sinusoid. It moved it.

Do this to every pair of Q and every pair of K, then run attention exactly as
02 section 4 describes. Nothing else changes - no bias term, no extra tensor, no
kernel modification. The score comes out as:

    q'_m . k'_n = sum over i of [ terms in (m - n) * w_i ]

Absolute position is gone from the result. Only the gap remains.

5.3 Three Changes at Once
-------------------------

Line RoPE up against note 03 and every axis has moved:

                        sinusoidal (2017)        RoPE (2021)
    applied to          the input embedding      Q and K
    how                 added                    rotated (multiplied)
    when                once, at the bottom      inside every attention call
    relative offset     recoverable in principle exact by construction

Each change fixes something specific, and the third is the one people underrate.

Applied to Q and K, not the embedding. Note 03 section 6.1 had to argue that
adding E + P is acceptable because a learned projection can pull them apart
again. RoPE never mixes them. The Value vectors are not rotated at all - what a
token CONTRIBUTES stays purely semantic, and position affects only how much
attention it receives. Note 02 section 3 separated the "match" role from the
"contribute" role; RoPE respects that separation, and sinusoidal PE, which
contaminates all three, does not.

Rotated, not added. A rotation preserves length. Note 03 section 4.2 observed
that every PE row has the same norm, which was nice; here it is load-bearing. The
magnitude of a query is untouched by where it sits, so position cannot make a
token shout or whisper. It can only change what the token points at.

Inside every attention call. This is the quiet one. Sinusoidal PE is injected
once and must then survive six or ninety-six layers, carried up by residual
connections (note 03 section 6.2) and diluted at every step by whatever else gets
added to the stream. Nobody guarantees layer 40 still has clean access to it.
RoPE re-applies the rotation at every layer, from the current Q and K. Position
is not a signal that decays through depth; it is re-stated every time it is used.

5.4 Why the Implementation Looks Wrong
--------------------------------------

Open any real RoPE implementation and the pairing will not be what section 5.2
described. Instead of pairing dimension 0 with 1, and 2 with 3, it pairs
dimension 0 with `d_model/2`, and 1 with `d_model/2 + 1` - the first half against
the second half. The code is usually a function called `rotate_half`.

The reason is purely mechanical. Pairing adjacent dimensions means interleaved
memory access; splitting the vector in half means the whole rotation is two slice
operations and one concatenation, which is far faster on a GPU.

It is mathematically the same thing, and the argument is worth following because
it recurs. The pairing is just a choice of which dimensions to call partners.
Q and K are produced by learned matrices `W_Q` and `W_K`, and those matrices can
permute their own output columns at no cost - a permutation is a valid setting of
the weights. So whichever pairing you fix, training finds the equivalent
solution. The convention has to be consistent between Q and K; beyond that it is
free.

5.5 Long-Range Decay, Honestly
------------------------------

RoPE is often credited with a built-in preference for nearby tokens. Note 03
section 5.2 gave the reason: the positional part of the score is a sum of
`cos(w_i * k)` terms, which is largest at k = 0 and tends to shrink as k grows.

The honest version: it shrinks with heavy oscillation, not smoothly, and the
envelope decays slowly. It is a mild tendency, nothing like ALiBi's enforced
ramp. Do not think of RoPE as "attention with a locality bias". Think of it as
attention with an exact notion of distance, which the model may use as it likes -
including ignoring it to retrieve something 24,000 tokens back. That freedom is
precisely what ALiBi gave up in section 4.2, and it is why RoPE, not ALiBi, is
the substrate of long-context models.

5.6 The Cache
-------------

One practical property decided a lot of adoption.

During generation, keys and values for tokens already produced are stored and
reused - the KV cache (12_inference.txt). For that to work, a cached key must
still be correct when a new token arrives.

With RoPE, the key for position 40 is rotated by `40 * w_i` when it is created,
and that is final. Position 41 arrives, gets rotated by `41 * w_i`, and the score
between them comes out depending on the gap of 1, automatically. Nothing cached
is ever revisited.

Contrast Shaw's scheme, where the addend depends on the PAIR: the correction
between the new token and a cached one cannot be baked into the cached key,
because it was not known when that key was stored. RoPE gets the relative
property while every stored quantity stays absolute and immutable. That is an
unusually elegant property, and it is why RoPE costs nothing at inference.

6. RoPE Hits the Same Wall
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RoPE makes the score depend only on the gap. Section 1 argued that this is
exactly what length generalization requires. So RoPE should extrapolate to any
length.

It does not. Push a RoPE model past its training length and it breaks much like
the 2017 model did.

This is the most instructive failure in the note, because the theory really is
correct and the conclusion really does not follow.

6.1 Where the Argument Leaks
----------------------------

The score depends only on the gap. Fine. But which GAPS did the model train on?

Trained at 2,048 tokens, the largest gap that ever occurred is 2,047. Gap 25,000
is not a familiar quantity appearing in an unfamiliar place. It is an unfamiliar
quantity. "Only depends on the gap" guarantees consistency across positions; it
guarantees nothing about gaps outside the training range.

And now section 1.2's asymmetry returns, in sharper form. Ask what each frequency
has experienced over gaps up to 2,048:

    wavelength 6         wrapped 341 times     every rotation angle, thoroughly
    wavelength 600       wrapped 3.4 times     every rotation angle
    wavelength 6,000     one third of a turn   only a slice
    wavelength 62,800    3% of a turn          a tiny arc

The fast pairs are genuinely safe. They have seen every angle they can produce,
so a gap of 25,000 rotates them to somewhere they have been many times.

The slow pairs have not. For them the rotation angle has only ever crept across a
small arc, and it has been monotonic - the angle grew with the gap, so the model
could read distance straight off it, like a dial that never wrapped. Extend to
gap 25,000 and that dial swings into a region the model has never read, and
eventually wraps, at which point a very large gap starts producing angles that
look like a small one.

So: the slow dimensions, the ones responsible for long-range structure, are
exactly the ones that cannot be trusted at long range. That is the whole problem,
and every fix in section 7 is a different answer to it.

6.2 Why Not Just Train Long
---------------------------

The obvious response is to train at 128k tokens from the start.

Attention is quadratic (02 section 8). Going from 2k to 128k is 64 times the
length and roughly 4,096 times the attention cost per document, on top of needing
a corpus of genuinely long documents, which is scarce - most text is short.

So long-context models are not trained long. They are trained short and then
STRETCHED, usually with a brief fine-tune at the target length. Making the
stretch work is one of the most consequential pieces of engineering in modern
LLMs, and it is entirely a story about the frequencies in section 6.1.

================================================================================

7. Stretching RoPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three ideas in sequence, each fixing the previous one's damage. This is a good
miniature of how the field actually moves.

7.1 Position Interpolation: Squash Everything
---------------------------------------------

The 2023 idea, and it is almost too simple. If the model is fine on positions
0-2,048 and you want 8,192, do not extrapolate. INTERPOLATE. Divide every
position by 4:

    pos' = pos * (L_train / L_target)

Position 8,000 becomes position 2,000. Every rotation angle the model ever sees
now lies inside the range it was trained on. Nothing is out of distribution. Add
a short fine-tune and it works - this is what took the first Llama models from
2k to 8k and beyond.

The cost is visible in the arithmetic. Adjacent tokens used to be 1 apart and are
now 0.25 apart. Every gap shrank by 4x, so every distinction the model could make
is now four times finer than the resolution it was trained to have. Tokens that
were clearly one apart become nearly indistinguishable.

And notice WHICH dimensions suffer. The fast pairs, the ones that resolve local
order, are squashed hardest in relative terms - and per section 6.1 they were the
dimensions that never needed help. PI protects the fragile slow dimensions by
crushing the healthy fast ones. It treats a targeted problem with a uniform
remedy.

7.2 NTK-Aware Scaling: Do Not Squash, Re-Tune
---------------------------------------------

The next idea, which surfaced in the open-source community before the literature,
leaves positions alone and changes the BASE instead:

    theta = 10000   ->   theta' = 10000 * s^(d/(d-2))     for extension factor s

Note 03 section 3.5 established that the base sets the longest wavelength.
Raising it stretches the whole ladder - but not evenly. Look at the frequency
formula: `w_i = 1 / theta^(2i/d_model)`. At i = 0 the exponent is 0, so the
fastest pair is completely unaffected by any change to theta. At the far end the
exponent is nearly 1, so the slowest pair is stretched by nearly the full factor.

That is the selectivity PI lacked, obtained for free from the shape of the
ladder. High frequencies keep their local resolution; low frequencies get their
wavelengths extended so they no longer wrap or wander off their trained arc. It
degrades gracefully even without fine-tuning.

The same insight explains a change you will see in model configs and might
otherwise read as arbitrary. Llama 2 used base 10,000; Llama 3 uses 500,000;
several long-context models use 1,000,000 or more. Those are not tuned magic
numbers. A larger base means longer wavelengths means the slow dimensions do not
wrap within the intended context, which is section 6.1's requirement stated as a
hyperparameter.

7.3 YaRN: Sort the Dimensions Explicitly
----------------------------------------

NTK scaling gets the right BEHAVIOUR - touch slow dimensions, spare fast ones -
as a side effect of the exponent. YaRN makes it the actual rule.

For each pair, compare its wavelength to the training context length and sort it
into three groups:

    wavelength << L_train    wrapped many times during training
                             safe to extrapolate; leave it completely alone

    wavelength >> L_train    never completed a cycle
                             fully interpolate it, PI-style

    in between               ramp smoothly between the two treatments

This is section 6.1 turned directly into an algorithm. No dimension is treated
because of where it sits in the ladder; each is treated according to whether it
personally saw enough rotation during training to be trusted.

YaRN adds one more correction that comes from section 1.2 rather than from
frequencies. A longer sequence means the softmax spreads its weight over more
competitors, so attention flattens for reasons that have nothing to do with
position. YaRN compensates by scaling the logits by a factor that grows slowly
with the extension factor - roughly `0.1 * ln(s) + 1`. It is a small detail with
a nice moral: two different things go out of distribution when you lengthen a
sequence, and fixing the positional one alone is not enough.

Llama 3.1's configuration is a close relative of this - a piecewise scaling with
separate treatment of low and high frequency bands, expressed as `low_freq_factor`
and `high_freq_factor` in its config. If you have ever read those fields and
wondered what they meant, they mean section 6.1.

7.4 The Shape of the Whole Section
----------------------------------

    PI    squash everything                 uniform, damages what was healthy
    NTK   raise the base                    selective as a side effect
    YaRN  sort by measured wavelength       selective on purpose, plus temperature

One problem, three answers, each more precisely aimed than the last. That
progression - blunt fix, structural fix, targeted fix - is the normal shape of
progress here, and it is worth recognizing because it repeats.

================================================================================

8. The Reversal: Maybe None of This Was Needed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything so far rests on a premise stated in note 03 section 1 and never
questioned: attention is permutation equivariant, therefore position must be
injected, therefore the only question is how.

For decoder-only models, that premise is false.

8.1 Where the Argument Went Wrong
---------------------------------

The permutation argument was made about bare self-attention. But a language model
does not run bare self-attention. It runs MASKED self-attention
(08_masked_multihead_attention.txt), and the mask is not symmetric.

Count what each position can see:

    position 0    sees 1 token
    position 1    sees 2 tokens
    position 2    sees 3 tokens
    position 7    sees 8 tokens

The number of visible tokens IS the position. Not encoded, not added - it is a
structural fact about the shape of the computation. Permute the sequence and the
mask does not permute with it, so the whole layer is no longer permutation
equivariant. The proof that motivated this entire line of work does not apply.

And a network can read that signal. Attention with near-uniform weights over
`t + 1` visible tokens produces an average whose relationship to the sum scales
with `1/(t+1)`; that quantity varies with position, and later layers can build on
it. The counting is approximate and emerges over several layers rather than being
handed over cleanly, but it is available.

8.2 What Happened When Someone Checked
--------------------------------------

Somebody trained decoder-only language models with no positional encoding at all -
NoPE - and probed them. The models had positional information internally, and
their language modelling quality was competitive.

The follow-up result is the striking one. On tasks that specifically test
generalizing to longer inputs than trained on, NoPE did not merely survive - it
matched or beat RoPE, ALiBi, and T5 bias. The models with nothing were better at
the exact thing every method in sections 2 through 7 was designed to achieve.

In hindsight it is not so strange. Every explicit scheme hands the model a
positional representation with a domain: a table with a range, a bucket that runs
out, an angle that wraps, a penalty calibrated on distances up to 2,048. Leaving
the sequence at length 2,048 is inside the domain; going to 30,000 is outside it.
NoPE has no representation, so it has no domain to leave. The counting the causal
mask provides works identically at any length, because it is not a representation
at all - it is the shape of the computation.

8.3 The Boundaries of the Claim
-------------------------------

Do not over-read this. Four qualifications.

It is causal-only. BERT and every encoder attends both ways, with no mask
breaking symmetry, so an encoder with no positional encoding genuinely cannot
distinguish "Moaz killed Lion" from "Lion killed Moaz". For bidirectional models
note 03's argument stands exactly as written.

The evidence is strongest at modest scale, on tasks chosen to isolate length
generalization. It is not a demonstration that frontier-scale models should drop
positional encoding.

Implicit position is imprecise. It emerges gradually across layers rather than
being available in layer 1, and it is a soft count rather than an exact index.
Tasks needing sharp positional discrimination are better served by RoPE.

The field did not switch. Essentially every production model still uses RoPE,
because at scale, with the extension methods of section 7, it works well and it
is precise.

8.4 The Interesting Compromise
------------------------------

The influence of NoPE shows up in hybrids rather than in wholesale adoption.
Several recent architectures apply RoPE to most layers and leave a subset with no
positional encoding at all - the RoPE layers supplying precise local ordering, the
NoPE layers giving the model a path for long-range attention that no rotation can
push out of distribution. Llama 4 has been described as using an interleaved
scheme of this kind, and Cohere's Command models interleave positionally-encoded
sliding-window layers with unencoded full-attention layers.

The idea that position should be handled differently in different LAYERS, rather
than uniformly through the stack, is where this is currently heading. That is
about as far as the story goes as of now.

9. Position Was Never One-Dimensional
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One more assumption to examine, held silently since note 03 section 1: that a
position is a single number, because text is a line of tokens.

An image is not a line. Split into patches, a patch is at row 4, column 7, and
flattening that grid into a sequence destroys the fact that the patch above it -
a full row away in the flattened index - is its immediate neighbour.

Video is worse: frame, row, column. Three coordinates, on wildly different scales
and with different meanings.

The fix is the natural one: stop spending the whole frequency ladder on one axis
and divide it. 2D RoPE gives half the dimension pairs to the row coordinate and
half to the column, each rotated by its own coordinate. Qwen2-VL's M-RoPE
(multimodal RoPE) splits three ways for video - temporal, height, width - and
sets text as the degenerate case where all three coordinates advance together.

The mechanism does not change at all. Section 5.1's requirement - dot product
depends only on the difference - holds per axis, so a rotation per axis gives
relative position per axis, and a patch can attend "two rows up" as cleanly as a
token attends "three tokens back". The only real design question is budget: how
many of your d_model/2 pairs each axis is worth.

Worth noticing that this only works because RoPE is a per-pair operation. A
scheme that consumed the whole vector at once would have to be redesigned for
each new dimensionality. Composability was not a design goal; it fell out of the
geometry.

10. Where It Stands
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Method       Enters at         Relative  Learned  Extrapolates
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------
    Sinusoidal   input embedding   no        no       poorly
    Learned      input embedding   no        yes      not at all
    Shaw         inside the score  yes       yes      crudely (clipped)
    T5 bias      the score         yes       yes      crudely (last bucket)
    ALiBi        the score         yes       no       yes, by suppressing range
    RoPE         Q and K           yes       no       with scaling (section 7)
    NoPE         nowhere           n/a       n/a      well, causal models only

If you are building something today: use RoPE, with a base sized for your target
context, and one of the scalings from section 7 if you intend to stretch it. Use
sinusoids only for a small encoder where simplicity matters more than range.

The larger point is the trajectory. Read the table top to bottom and position
moves steadily later in the computation and steadily closer to the thing that
needs it - from a vector added to the input, to a correction on the score, to a
transformation of the operands themselves, to nothing at all. Each step removed
an assumption someone had stopped noticing they were making.

The current open questions follow the same pattern. Should position be uniform
across layers, or should some layers have none (section 8.4)? Can a model be
trained short and used long without any fine-tune? Is the rotation the right
group, or just the first one anybody solved for? None of these are settled.

11. Practice Questions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: A sinusoidal model trained on 2,048 tokens is given 30,000. Sinusoids are
    defined at every position, and PE(pos).PE(pos+k) depends only on k. So why
    does it fail?
A:  Both facts are about PE alone, and the model never sees PE alone. It sees
    E + PE through learned projections, and those projections were fitted on the
    positions training contained. Concretely, dimensions whose wavelength exceeds
    2,048 never completed a cycle, so they only ever occupied a small arc of
    their range; beyond 2,048 they move into angles never seen. Separately, the
    softmax must now divide attention among fifteen times as many competitors,
    which is out of distribution for reasons unrelated to position.

Q2: Which positional dimensions are safe to extrapolate and which are not, and
    why is that the opposite of intuition?
A:  Fast (short-wavelength) dimensions are safe: they wrapped hundreds of times
    during training, so every angle they can produce was seen. Slow
    (long-wavelength) dimensions are fragile: they never completed a cycle, so
    their trained range is a small arc. The intuition trap is that the slow
    dimensions are the ones meant to carry long-range information, so the
    machinery for long distances is exactly the machinery that fails at long
    distances.

Q3: Shaw's relative encoding was better than absolute PE and was not adopted.
    Why not?
A:  It adds a distinct learned vector per token PAIR inside the dot product, so
    scores are no longer one matrix multiplication. Naively it materializes an
    (n, n, d_k) tensor - a factor of d_k on top of the score matrix, 64x at
    d_k = 64. The idea was right; the price per pair was not payable.

Q4: What did T5 change about Shaw's scheme, and what capability did that cost?
A:  It replaced the per-pair vector with a single learned scalar per
    (bucket, head), added to the score after Q K^T. That keeps attention as one
    matmul and costs about 32 numbers per head. The cost is that a scalar added
    regardless of content cannot express anything depending on both the gap and
    what the two tokens are, which Shaw's in-product vector could.

Q5: ALiBi extrapolates to sequences far past its training length. Why is that
    still not a solution to long context?
A:  It extrapolates by suppression. Every head has a fixed slope, so distant
    tokens are penalized without bound and each head effectively reads a sliding
    window. That is an excellent prior for next-word prediction and exactly wrong
    for retrieving a clause 24,000 tokens back. It survives long inputs; it does
    not use them.

Q6: RoPE is derived rather than proposed. What is the requirement, and why does
    rotation satisfy it?
A:  Requirement: find f such that <f(q,m), f(k,n)> depends only on q, k and
    (m - n). Rotation satisfies it because a dot product depends only on lengths
    and the angle between two vectors. Rotating by m*w and n*w changes the angle
    between them by exactly (m - n)*w, so absolute positions cancel and the
    result is a function of the gap alone.

Q7: Name the three axes on which RoPE differs from sinusoidal PE, and give the
    specific benefit of each.
A:  (i) Applied to Q and K rather than the embedding - meaning and position are
    never mixed, and V stays unrotated so what a token contributes is purely
    semantic. (ii) Rotation rather than addition - length is preserved, so
    position cannot make a token louder, only redirect it. (iii) Re-applied every
    layer rather than injected once - position does not have to survive depth via
    residual connections.

Q8: Why does RoPE cost nothing at inference with a KV cache, while Shaw's scheme
    would?
A:  RoPE rotates each key by its own absolute position when the key is created,
    so a cached key is final and the relative property emerges automatically when
    a later query is scored against it. Shaw's correction depends on the pair, so
    it cannot be folded into a key at storage time - it was unknown then.

Q9: RoPE makes the score depend only on the gap. Why does that not deliver
    unlimited length generalization?
A:  Because it guarantees consistency ACROSS positions, not validity for unseen
    GAPS. Trained at 2,048, the largest gap ever encountered is 2,047; a gap of
    25,000 is a new input. For the slow pairs, which never completed a rotation,
    it puts the angle in territory never trained and eventually wraps it, so a
    very large gap can be confused with a small one.

Q10: Position Interpolation works but blunts the model. What exactly does it
     damage, and how do NTK scaling and YaRN avoid that?
A:  PI divides every position by the extension factor, so all gaps shrink
     uniformly and local resolution is lost - and the dimensions that lose it are
     the fast ones, which never needed help. NTK scaling raises the base instead;
     because the exponent 2i/d_model is 0 for the fastest pair and near 1 for the
     slowest, fast pairs are untouched and slow ones stretched. YaRN makes that
     explicit, classifying each pair by whether its wavelength is shorter than the
     training length (leave alone), longer (interpolate), or in between (ramp).

Q11: Why does Llama 3 use a RoPE base of 500,000 instead of 10,000?
A:  The base sets the longest wavelength. A larger base lengthens the slow
     dimensions so they do not wrap or leave their trained arc within the intended
     context window. It is section 6.1's requirement expressed as a
     hyperparameter, not a tuned constant.

Q12: Note 03 argued position must be injected because attention is permutation
     equivariant. Why does that argument fail for decoder-only models?
A:  They use a causal mask, which is not symmetric. Position t sees exactly t + 1
     tokens, so the number of visible tokens is itself positional information, and
     permuting the sequence does not permute the mask. The layer is not
     permutation equivariant, so the premise does not hold. It does still hold for
     bidirectional encoders, which have no such mask.

Q13: NoPE models can beat RoPE at length generalization. Give the intuition, and
     state the limits of the result.
A:  Every explicit scheme supplies a positional representation with a domain - a
     table range, a final bucket, an angle that wraps - and going long leaves that
     domain. NoPE has no representation and so no domain to leave; the counting
     the causal mask provides behaves identically at any length. Limits: causal
     models only, evidence strongest at modest scale on length-generalization
     tasks, the implicit signal is soft and emerges over several layers, and
     production models still use RoPE.

Q14: What has to change in RoPE to handle images or video, and why is the change
     so small?
A:  Only the allocation of pairs. Give some dimension pairs the row coordinate
     and others the column (and others the frame index), each rotated by its own
     coordinate. The mechanism is untouched because RoPE acts independently on
     each 2D pair, and section 5.1's guarantee holds per axis - so relative
     position per axis comes out automatically. The only real decision is how many
     pairs each axis deserves.
